# Generated battle-story editorial review — 27 September 2026

**Decision: hold the pilot in draft.** Structural validity and synthetic roster
rendering pass. Mission fidelity does not. These 22,770 possible matchups are
combinations of 88 mission incidents and army descriptions, not independently
authored or editorially approved stories. Keep the existing written-story
completion requirement and the published application unchanged.

## Method and limits

I read all three paragraphs and all three alternative endings in the fixed
110-scene sample generated by
`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/sample-generated-game-stories.mts`.
It covers five matchups per mission, including one mirror, every role, and all
four incident seeds per mission. The fifth scene per mission repeats one
incident with different participants. I compared mission premises with the
[official ITS season 18 scenario index](https://experience.corvusbelli.com/en/infinity/its)
and its linked scenario objectives where available. Two catalog missions,
Dead Man's Switch and Panic Room, are absent from that season's index and
require a pinned historical scenario reference before editorial approval.

This is a targeted editorial read, not a blinded panel, a random sample of all
matchups, or evidence that prose accurately describes actual player actions.
The roster checks use synthetic decoded lists, not live submitted games.

| Dimension | Standard for approval | Observed in this pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | Three distinct 40–75-word paragraphs; supported placeholders and three distinct one-sentence endings | Passes structural validator over 22,770 pairs × four incidents × three roles (273,240 composed scenes). |
| Roster and outcome | A game-linked eligible model and correct participant names and ending for wins, losses, draws | Synthetic roster tests now render all 22 missions × four incidents × three roles × three results (792 renders), including placeholder and ending assertions; real submission integration is untested. |
| Chronology | An action may lead coherently to any one of the three endings | Eight seed actions or openings were corrected during this review; the checker cannot prove every remaining causal chain. |
| Mission fidelity | Plot and objectives fit the named mission and the applicable version of its rules | **Fails:** multiple incidents tell stories about objects absent from their mission objectives. Examples and official sources below. |
| Originality and voice | Incident, faction and role materially affect the story; language does not feel repetitive across a reading session | **Unapproved:** four incident plots per mission are reused across its 1,035 army pairings; the 110-scene sample has 88 distinct middle paragraphs. Faction changes usually alter a crew label and approach, not the event. |

### 4. Area of Interest — Invincible Army vs. White Company (objective)

**Editorial note:** Mission mismatch: boundary measurement substitutes for area control and antenna activation.

A step in the wrong direction would leave the nearest post undefended.

The runoff carried survey tags into a drain whose grate lay across the open firing lane. Rounds struck the survey stakes, so neither crew could trust the line painted between them. One tag caught under the grate, still showing enough ink to settle the first measurement.

{{hero}} reached the drain, read the soaked tag through the grate, and checked it against the older curb mark. Another sheet of rain blurred the paint but left the shallow engraving visible. The other patrol pushed along the curb before the recovered measurement could be shared. The grate shook under the runoff as the last tag disappeared into the dark below.

- Hero wins: {{heroPlayer}}’s patrol held the engraved mark and transmitted the recovered boundary measurement.
- Hero loses: {{otherPlayer}}’s patrol seized the readable tag and claimed the newly exposed curb.
- Draw: Both patrols saw the older mark, but the final measurement washed into the drain.
